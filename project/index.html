Helios

A voice device for the desk, and the 8.3-billion-parameter language model behind it. Both built from scratch by one student.

Scroll to follow the build

The device

A small printed case with a screen, a speaker and a sun on each side.

Designed in Autodesk Fusion and 3D printed in PETG. Gold sun inlays sit on both sides.

  • Width × height × depth136 × 110 × 87 mm
  • Design filesFusion, version 4

Design process

Four versions since October.

Each version came from what the last one got wrong. Shown left to right: v1, v3 and v4.

  • v1Printed Oct 26. The speaker slid loose, the cords had no room, and it looked like an old TV.
  • v2Oct 26–27. No chin, the speaker firing down, tungsten weights in the screen ledge. Set aside.
  • v3Oct 29. v1's inside on a low plinth, with a 204 g steel plate sliding in from the back as the weight.
  • v4Nov 10. A fresh start from v1: the speaker clamped down, three cord clips, the mic board on two screws.

Testing

Checked with numbers before it was printed.

A tap at the top of the screen should not tip it over. A tipping model set the case depth: 56 mm with nothing added, instead of 48 mm with tungsten putty in the corners.

  • Tap it must survive1.5 N
  • Depth 48 mm, no putty1.55 N
  • Depth 56 mm, no putty1.89 N

Inside

Everything fits in one small box.

A screen, a computer, a microphone board and a speaker, held by printed clamps, posts and cord clips.

The screen

A 5-inch touchscreen up front.

It shows the time, the sun's path through the day, and what Helios is doing: listening, thinking or answering.

  • DisplayHosyond 5″ DSI
  • Resolution800 × 480

The computer

A Raspberry Pi 4 runs the device.

It listens for the wake word, turns speech into text with Moonshine, and speaks answers with Piper.

  • BoardRaspberry Pi 4
  • Speech to textMoonshine
  • Text to speechPiper

Ears and voice

Microphones on top, a speaker below.

The ReSpeaker Lite board on top is the microphone that hears “Hey Helios.” A 3 W speaker sits under the screen, behind the front grille.

  • MicrophonesReSpeaker Lite
  • SpeakerMakerHawk 3 W, 8 Ω

One question

The thinking happens on a second machine.

The device sends each question over the network to an NVIDIA DGX Spark, which runs the full model.

  • PiHears “Hey Helios” and turns the question into text.
  • SparkLooks up live facts (weather, Wikipedia, news) and ranks them with BM25.
  • SparkHelios reads those facts and writes the answer.
  • PiPiper speaks the answer out loud.

Inside the model

24 layers, 12 experts in each.

Every word passes up through all 24 layers. In each layer a router picks 2 of the 12 experts, so only a fifth of the model works on any one word.

  • Parameters8.28 B total, 1.68 B active
  • Experts12 per layer, top 2
  • Context4,096 tokens
  • Tokenizerown, 50,304 tokens

Training

Trained from scratch on 15.3 billion tokens.

66 days on one DGX Spark, from Aug 18 to Oct 23. In November it was fine-tuned on conversations in a spoken style, so it answers like a voice assistant.

  • Steps58,300
  • Loss, step 1,750 → end3.15 → 1.90
  • Speed, before → after tuning1,712 → 2,874 tok/s
