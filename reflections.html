What was hard, and how I got past it

Hurdle

It would confidently make things up

How

A model this size can't reliably memorize facts. So instead of trusting its memory, I look up real information and hand it to the model before it answers.

Hurdle

I can't build a device around an unfinished AI

How

I built a stand-in that acts like it. The device gets built and tested now, and the real model swaps in later.

Hurdle

I almost over-built the hardware

How

A custom circuit board would have cost weeks and real risk. I caught it early and switched to off-the-shelf parts: same result, far less risk.

Sprint 4 · Oct 12 – 23

Checking my own work saved two expensive mistakes

What landed
  • Pre-training finished Oct 23 after 66 days; weights exported, verified and backed up
  • A 42,052-conversation dataset in Helios's voice, made for free on my own Mac
  • The case redesigned around a researched sound horn
  • 88 design tests that caught bolt posts not attached to the case, before printing
What didn't
  • The fine-tuning script I committed to wasn't written yet
  • The case wasn't printed, because my own review sent it back for fixes
  • Both carried into Sprint 5
Process change: I moved the whole project into one organized notebook, with a task list and a daily log, and push to GitHub as I go. Documenting the same day replaced rebuilding it from memory.

Sprint 6 change

Every GitHub push gets an Unrulr post

I pushed to GitHub every day I worked (15 commits, 8 daily logs) but posted to Unrulr only on the last day. Tying the two together means the journey is written as it happens. The check: days with a push but no post. The goal is zero.

What I'm carrying forward

Lessons so far

Plan for failure before it happens. A written fallback for every risk turns a bad day into a pre-made decision instead of panic.

Don't wait on the slow part. Building the device against a stand-in model meant two months of training weren't two months of waiting.

Check the work honestly. Testing the data and the design before using them caught a broken print and thousands of bad answers.

Talk to the model. Its loss numbers looked fine. Chatting with it is what showed the bad habits.

My own sign-off can be the bottleneck. The longest delay in Sprint 5 was me not approving finished work. Decide, then move.
